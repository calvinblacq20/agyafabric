(01) THE HOUSE

Every cloth here is made by a hand you could shake. We buy the yarn, dress the loom, cut the stamp and mix the ink. Nothing leaves this house until it has been washed, pressed and folded the way our grandmothers folded cloth in Ghana.

WHAT WE SELL Full cloths of twelve yards, half pieces of six, single kente strips, and made-to-measure work from the atelier in Osu.

WHERE IT COMES FROM Bonwire for kente, Ntonso for adinkra, Daboya for fugu, Aburi for indigo. We pay the weavers directly, in full, on collection.

HOW LONG IT TAKES A plain strip takes two days on the loom. A royal cloth of twenty-four strips takes a weaver about eleven weeks.

(03) THE MAKING

From yarn
to folded cloth.

01

Yarn

Cotton from Tamale, rayon and silk from the market at Kejetia. Dyed in the yard behind the house.

02

Warping

Threads walked out along the warping frame — up to four hundred yards for a single royal cloth.

03

Weaving

Four inches at a time on the double-heddle loom. Warp-faced blocks and weft-faced blocks alternate.

04

Stamping

Calabash stamps cut by hand, pressed into badie bark ink. Every symbol carries a proverb.

05

Sewing

Strips joined edge to edge so the blocks break joint and the pattern keeps its beat across the cloth.

06

Finishing

Washed cold, pressed, folded in twelve, wrapped in calico and tied with a strip of the same cloth.
