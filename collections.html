(01) BONWIRE

Kente, four inches at a time.

Woven on the double-heddle loom in Bonwire, Ashanti. Strips come off the loom four inches wide and are hand-sewn edge to edge, offset so the blocks break joint across the cloth.

A

Adweneasa

Twenty-four strips, fully patterned. The weaver’s showpiece. Eleven weeks.

B

Oyokoman

Gold, green and maroon. Worn for durbars and the naming of a child.

C

Babadua

Fine stacked bars in the weft. A quieter cloth, sold by the half piece.

(02) NTONSO

Every stamp is a sentence.

Adinkra symbols are cut into calabash, dipped in badie bark ink boiled down for three days, and pressed into panels combed out with a wooden rake. Gye Nyame, Sankofa, Dwennimmen — each one carries a proverb.

A

Kuntunkuni

The dark mourning cloth. Stamped on cotton dyed with tree bark.

B

Nwomu

White ground, black ink. Worn to funerals of the very old, where the life was full.

C

Indigo panels

Our own run: adinkra stamped over an indigo-dyed base. Limited to forty pieces.

(03) ACCRA WAX

Wax, then the crackle.

Printed in our own yard off Oxford Street. Hot wax laid by stamp and brush, cloth dyed cold, wax cracked before the second bath so the dye runs into the fractures. No two lengths repeat.

A

Six yards

The standard cut for a kaba and slit. Sold folded, in calico.

B

Twelve yards

Full cloth with enough left for a head wrap and a child’s piece.

C

Off-cuts

Half and quarter yards from the end of the run, priced by weight.

(04) ABURI INDIGO — (05) DABOYA FUGU

Tied, stitched, sunk in the vat.

Indigo from Aburi is tied with raffia and stitched before dyeing, so the resist leaves rings and rays where the dye could not reach. Fugu from Daboya arrives as narrow woven strips, checked in indigo and white, and is cut for smocks in Osu.

A

Bosommuru indigo

Four dips, dried between each. The blue keeps deepening for a week after.

B

Damba fugu

Strip cloth for the full smock, with the deep pocket and the loose sleeve.

C

Sapei

A finer northern check, cut for shirts rather than the full smock.
